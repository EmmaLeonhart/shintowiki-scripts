# Wiki 403 audit — why did Miraheze start blocking us on 2026-07-11?

*Audit run 2026-07-11 (late UTC), in response to the top-of-queue directive. Reports
expire after a week (see CLAUDE.md); this is a working artifact, not an archive.*

## Question

Emma's hypothesis: "we probably fucked it up" — a change we made in the last few days
raised our request rate to shinto.miraheze.org enough to trip the anti-DDoS block, and
that's why it started **today** rather than at other times.

## What the block actually is (confirmed live)

A direct read-only GET to `https://shinto.miraheze.org/w/api.php` right now returns:

```
HTTP 403
<title>Checking your connection …</title>
<meta name="description" content="Our systems have detected unusual activity.
Your connection is being securely checked.">
```

This is Miraheze's Cloudflare-style **managed challenge** page — the same one
`check_wiki_login.py` detects. Per [[reference_miraheze_antiddos_challenge]] it hits
**programmatic and browser clients alike**, is intermittent, and is "not a UA fix; retry
later." The "unusual activity" wording is Cloudflare boilerplate, not a per-IP verdict.

The block appeared **between 00:11 and 03:44 UTC**, inside a *low-activity* window: the
only wiki-touching run in that gap was a single Independent Pages Sync at 00:59. There
was **no request-volume spike of ours immediately before the block.**

Things that are commonly blamed but are **victims, not causes** (they ran *after* onset):

- **cleanup-loop** (the big daily orchestrator sweep) ran at **05:23 UTC** on 07-11 and
  failed — 1h40m *after* the block already started. It did not trigger it.
- **empty-items-report** first (slow, ~50-min) version landed **19:41 UTC** on 07-11 —
  ~16h *after* onset. Not the trigger (though a 50-min read loop against an already-live
  challenge is exactly the kind of thing that *prolongs* being flagged; it was fixed the
  same afternoon to a 0.7s Special:Export path).

## Did our baseline volume creep up recently? Yes — but 5 days before, not today

Real increases landed **2026-07-06**, not 07-11:

- **`git-synced-sync` `WIKI_EDIT_LIMIT` 100 → 500** (commit `7ac031ff`) — 5× the write
  ceiling per run, to drain a 4,608-page property-dump backlog. Scheduled 4×/hour
  (`5,20,35,50 * * * *`); GitHub actually fires it ~1×/hour.
- **`strip-property-dumps` new, every 2h** (commit `1a7310b4`) — new recurring drain of
  `[[Category:sync these pages now]]`.

Landed **today** pre-block: nothing heavy. The only 07-11 addition before onset would have
been the hourly **`wiki-editing-gate`** login check — but that is a single GET/hour and it
was committed at 14:27 PDT (21:27 UTC), *after* onset anyway.

## Conclusion

**The evidence does not support "a change we made today spiked our request rate and tripped
the block."** The block began in a quiet window with no burst of ours preceding it, and it
matches the known intermittent Miraheze-side managed challenge that also blocks browsers.
The most likely trigger is **Miraheze-side**, not us.

**But two things are still true and worth acting on:**

1. Our *baseline* wiki request volume did step up on 2026-07-06 (500-edit `git-synced-sync`
   runs + new 2h `strip-property-dumps`). This didn't spike at block-time, but it raises our
   steady-state footprint and makes us a fatter target for / slower to clear such challenges.
2. **Continuing to hammer the wiki while the challenge is up keeps us flagged.**
   `git-synced-sync` (~hourly) and `fandom-sync` (every 15 min) kept firing into the 403 all
   day. Even failing, those are requests against a WAF that is watching for exactly that.

Both points argue for the same fix Emma already directed: a **hard editing gate that makes
every wiki job bail while blocked**, plus a week-long lockout if EmmaBot can't get an edit
through. That both trims our footprint and stops the retry-hammering that prolongs the flag.
The gate is worth building **regardless of the trigger** — which is exactly the call Emma
made.
